# Records: physical player

This version replaces the earlier provider-driven turntable with a local groove simulation adapted from [Guillermo Rauch's vinyl](https://github.com/rauchg/vinyl), pinned at `bcc9264509d78ccbf4a449618f19c1cc3104b49c`. One renderer and one physical engine share metre-scale geometry. The physical deck has its own audio engine; explicitly selected service players are a separate listening mode.

## Listening contract

Polygondwanaland is the default: taking out its demo cuts the complete The Castle In The Air after a visitor gesture. The optional house pressing is a three-track synthesised demo from the upstream MIT-licensed code. Nothing downloads or cuts automatically on page load. Local audio files are decoded, cut into a stereo groove and passed to an AudioWorklet. They are never uploaded, persisted or associated silently with a commercial album. The label and source badge use the files' own names.

Ten curated sleeves contain cover art, factual context, numbered track lists and a listening panel. Nine have official Spotify album embeds; Masana Temples also has the label’s full-album YouTube upload, and Natural Light has Bandcamp. A player loads only after the visitor chooses it. Official player APIs connect play/pause, seeking and supported volume to the deck; the visible player handles account requirements and unsupported controls. No iframe load event is claimed as successful audio playback. Spotify may supply previews; YouTube availability is video/account/region dependent. Direct service links remain visible for failures and full listening. Choosing a service suspends physical audio; camera changes retain the visible player. Closing or changing a sleeve also closes its player. Browsing without choosing a player leaves the physical pressing alone. No stream is extracted or processed through the groove engine.

Groove wall displacement drives the stylus/contact model, cartridge and playback equalization. Motor speed, pitch, cue lift, hand contact, tracking force, anti-skate and record condition affect this model. Dust and scratches alter groove geometry; they are not an unrelated noise overlay. This is a newly simulated pressing from a digital source, not a reconstruction of an original vinyl master or a laboratory-validated model of a particular deck.

## Interaction and visual design

The camera freely orbits, pans and zooms. Room, Records, Deck and Needle offer smooth focus moves; Reset recovers the starting view. Reduced motion makes preset moves and sleeve selection immediate. The platter still reflects the physical audio source while playing.

Objects are handled; empty space moves the camera. Mouse/touch drags handle the arm, platter, pitch slider, anti-skate dial and output-volume knob. Volume appears in the dock when the active source supports it. Two fingers always control the camera, safely releasing any object hold. A background sleeve tap brings it forward; tapping the front sleeve takes out an available record or opens its listening choices. Tapping it during physical playback opens details without stopping the record. A horizontal fan drag browses without wrapping at either end. Previous/next buttons also browse and disable at the collection ends. The Look only button lets any one-finger drag move the camera. Wheel zoom requires the focused canvas or Ctrl/Meta; ordinary unfocused scrolling remains page scrolling. A visible Collection link exits the touch surface.

DOM controls provide motor, cue, speed, automatic placement, track cueing and adjustments. The canvas has keyboard orbit/zoom controls; all listening and album information has an HTML alternative. Cover textures remain full colour; interface typography and colors use the site's print/CRT design. Materials use physical metal, paper and acrylic values.

The fine 0.26 mm cantilever ends in a separate tapered diamond. Its 18 µm seating centre is pinned to the engine's `stylusXY` and contact height. The normal Needle view uses a low close-up angle and contact occlusion that fades on cue lift; its earlier waveform overlay is removed. A regression checks actual diamond mesh vertices as well as the head transform. The pitch cap, cue lever and counterweight move with their controls. The record shader reads visualization data from the actual cut. New pressings wait for the motor to coast down and the needle to clear before replacing the disc. An interrupted audio context or a 12-second recovery deadline installs the cut into an atomically stopped, raised and parked mechanism; a later tap resumes audio. Idle GPU rendering stops after mechanical motion settles; audio simulation continues while its context is active. Offscreen/hidden stages do not render, and hiding releases held objects.

### Minimal interface and handling pass

The flat interface is one contextual dock and a camera bar. Sleeve, Press and Tune open one secondary drawer at a time; phone drawers stack below the scene. The selected sleeve is presented ahead of its neighbours, hover peeks are distinct from selection, and extraction/placement/return follow the jacket opening and spindle. See [interaction design](records-interaction.md).

## Needle motion and physical-control feedback

The separate Grooves microscope view has been removed. The regular Needle close-up shows cartridge height, cantilever flex and radial tip displacement from the physical solver, following the same warped pressing that generates audio. No synthetic oscillation is added.

Tracking force and anti-skate were already active in the solver. Live inner/outer wall loads, contact loss and plinth displacement now make their consequences inspectable. A clean record may still track at several settings without dramatic audible damage. The knob drives output gain, not an invented cartridge property. Light, firm and heavy knocks feed the existing damped plinth model; the rendered platter/plinth now reflect its real displacement without magnification. No fake shake or added crackle is used.

## Architecture and data

Jekyll remains the only site build. `vinyl: true` gates the page stylesheet and native ES module. No package dependencies or framework are added.

- `main.js`: page state, mastering lifecycle, controls and telemetry/render bridge.
- `listening.js`: click-gated visible service players, full native recordings, track selection and permanent handoff links.
- `audio.js`: gesture activation, worklet lifecycle and one-time transfer of groove walls.
- `micro-surface.js`: sampled V-wall mesh and magnified contact rendering.
- `input.js`: object/camera gesture ownership and equivalent pointer behavior.
- `fan.js`, `deck-markings.js`: sleeves, back-cover track lists and physical control markings.
- `physics/`: pinned MIT engine, renderer, DSP, mastering worker and demo; provenance and local changes are listed in its README.

`_data/records.json` feeds the page, `/records.json` and the full-text export. Ten albums, 100 tracks. The first six retain their previously inspected streaming editions; *Excitable Boy* includes four bonus tracks. Four additions were chosen from Jonas's backlog under his instruction: *Dying Surfer Meets His Maker*, *Masana Temples*, *Kind of Blue* (five-track edition), and *Polygondwanaland*. Apple catalogue metadata and covers were retrieved with his explicit permission. Provenance URLs are stored alongside those entries. No private inventory, agent ranking or invented personal memories are published.

Local cutting is capped at 3 minutes/20 MB on small screens and 10 minutes/80 MB on larger screens. Mono inputs share one channel buffer. File metadata is checked before full decoding, then decoded duration is checked again. These are conservative input limits, not a mobile-memory guarantee. Cancellation invalidates unfinished jobs; the prior pressing remains available. Worklet wall arrays are transferred, not duplicated. A fresh cut is made when re-pressing.

## Default record and handling (v8)

Polygondwanaland is first in the crate and catalogue. The complete official The Castle In The Air (2:47) is the default physical demo, within both device limits. Nothing downloads until the visitor takes it out. Click/tap its 3D sleeve or Take out demo record; the source is fetched and cut, the disc slides up out of its sleeve, and it waits in hand. Click/tap the platter or Place on platter to seat it, then Put it on starts the real motor and cue. Once the disc is on the platter, clicking its sleeve opens the album details without stopping playback. Return record to sleeve explicitly stops and returns it. Its pressing is reused on the next extraction. The synthetic house record remains a secondary test option; all ten full recordings and service fallbacks remain available. Albums without reusable source audio open their listening options rather than claiming a physical pressing.

placement.js owns the handling poses in metres, separate from the groove engine. Transport and arm/platter manipulation are gated until the disc is seated. The moving disc uses the same edge/surface meshes and cut-derived texture as the seated record; it rises clear of the crate, moves above the deck and lowers vertically onto the spindle. Reduced motion shortens each move to its settled pose. The source is identified as a single-track pressing, not the whole album pressed into three minutes.

V8 muted Chromium checks use actual 3D sleeve and platter pointer clicks, then verify physical program audio, return and re-use, no initial media request, touch-emulated phone sleeve taps, reduced motion and default-demo selection after browsing another album.
