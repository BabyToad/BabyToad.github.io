DIV 00-B

Look test: Babel at 06:02

Blockwork room, Cycles city through the glass · Jonas's picks: route A, blue morning

Jonas asked for a city "super sick and realistic" behind the pixel room. The room still renders at 640 × 360 and is scaled 3× with nearest filtering. The city is pre-rendered in Blender Cycles from Babel's own data and drawn at native resolution behind the world view. The world SubViewport is transparent where the curtain-wall glass is, so the city is sharp and the room is pixel art. Both frames below are single 1920 × 1080 captures of that composite from Godot (tools/art/look_city.tscn).

L-5The plate: one strip, three depth layers

What the plate is made of

  • tools/art/city_blender.py (Blender 5.0, Cycles, headless) reads the Dark City Shining Babel setting data: 42 named buildings, 1,219 procgen buildings, streets, roads, tramway, sea wall, ridge and sea.
  • Buildings: tall towers get setbacks and crowns; the Pinnacle gets its spire and strobe.
  • Facades: a procedural window grid in world metres, per building type. Each window is lit or dark, with a room colour, a ceiling-to-floor gradient, blinds, a lamp pool or a TV. Temperature, tone and how many rooms are lit are set per building (a face attribute). Dark glass reflects the sky, and each floor has a spandrel band.
  • Roofs: AC units, water tanks, antennas with red beacons, rooftop billboards.
  • Neon: blade signs and strips on the Town and Lux faces. Holo ads carry Opticals' CODEX copy (OH-DI, NERPS!, KODAMA, HIGH WATER WE ARE HIRING).
  • Streets and roads glow sodium, with head- and tail-light dashes. The tramway is lit, and the sea wall carries lamps; boats sit on the sea.
  • Air and water: height and distance fog computed per camera ray (street mist low, violet haze high), a storm cloud deck in the world shader, and a glossy sea with wave bumps.
  • Afterwards city_post.py adds bloom (Pillow).
  • No image textures, no downloads, no generators; the only font is the DejaVu the game already ships.

One colour script with the film (agreed with Opticals)

  • 06:02, sun 2.5° up at bearing 120°, colour #ffb070. The camera looks west-north-west over the core, away from the sun, in both the film and the game.
  • Earth shadow #1c2436 under a Belt of Venus #b5687e, storm blue #22386a, zenith #0b1733. Clouds are dark storm blue with undersides lit #ffb070.
  • Windows are three parts sodium #ffb35c to one part #bfe6ff. Neon is PV orange #ff6a1f and magenta #ff3d8b. HW cyan #1fd6ea appears only on Tower 3 and High Water ads.
  • The room's section moods (night, flicker, blackout) are unchanged; only the city is at dawn.

Budgets

  • Dev PC GPU: one layer per Blender process, 4096 × 1580, 64 samples with the OptiX denoiser, and a pause between runs. Times: far 17 s, mid 23 s, near 15 s, composite 37 s. The whole city set takes about 1.5 min of GPU time. There were no long batches.
  • Web (computed, not yet measured in an export): in the frame, one plate pixel is 0.9 native px at 1080p, so a 3072-wide strip covers 1080p. In VRAM-compressed form (S3TC/BPTC on desktop Web), the three layers come to about 11 MB of VRAM and a similar download before pck compression. As lossy WebP, the download is about 0.5 MB, but each 3072 layer takes about 19 MB of VRAM once decoded (RGBA8). The mid and near layers crop to their alpha bounds. I'll measure both in the Web export before choosing.
  • Frame cost: three textured quads behind the world view, plus the transparent SubViewport. The look test drew 241–454 calls in the 3D view.

Next, on art-lab, in steps

  1. The backdrop node for game_root (Readout's hybrid already draws the world as a scaled SubViewport): plate layers behind it, parallax from the level camera, transparent where there is glass. Web export measurement.
  2. Animated overlays at native resolution: traffic streams on the roads, aircraft and drone lights, beacon blinks, window flicker through a mask, holo-ad loops, a searchlight, rain in front. These shaders join the Warmup.
  3. Blockwork production: blockwork.gdshader into Pal/LevelKit, Debris and BrickWall in local space, the prop kit into the level, the Kit.sign() restyle, PV orange in one line of Pal, and the section moods.

Rulings needed

  • Tower 3's height. The data has Tower 3 at 160 m (32 units), which can't hold Floors 70–75. The plate puts Floor 70 at about 255 m, above the Lux towers around it, so the view clears them. The data should raise Tower 3 to about 275 m; Opticals flagged the same.
  • The horizon is placed artistically. It sits about 60 % up the glass so the skyline clears the gallery deck. Physically it would sit at eye height, about 1.6 m.

Prop pass: before and after

DIV 00-D

Crisp lettering, and where the blur was

Jonas: "the pixelation makes fonts rough and a few things blur"

Captured at 2560 × 1440 (a 3× world) and at 1700 × 1000 (a non-integer window, 2× world). The world scale is always a whole number with nearest filtering, no MSAA, FXAA or TAA is on, and Blockwork, the props and the rigs are crisp. The blur was the lettering. Signs and posters baked their text into 64 px/m textures inside the 640 × 360 world, where a linear filter shrank them to 30 px/m before the 3–4× scale-up. Now the world keeps each sign's panel, frame and housing (pixel art) plus an invisible text mark, and SignText sets the lettering at native resolution in the sign's own font, projected onto the sign by the level camera. The mark decides per pixel whether the text shows, so a thug in front hides the lettering along with the panel. Lit signs take their light from the world pixel under each glyph.

Blur audit

  • World scale: a whole number with nearest filtering at every window size, crisp. At a non-integer window the world drops to the next whole scale and centres.
  • AA: none set, so no MSAA, FXAA or TAA.
  • Sign and poster text: fixed (above).
  • Blockwork: the only derivative it takes is the one-pixel rim, with no texture sampling; crisp.
  • Prop meshes: greedy quads carry one colour each, so nothing interpolates; crisp.
  • City plates: they were scaled 1.2× up at 4×. Fixed: re-exported at 120 px/m, +1.6 MB S3TC.
  • Not ours: the HUD at fractional UI scales (Readout's layout).

Web cost (Chrome, 2560 × 1440)

  • Video memory dropped from 191 to 125 MB, because the baked sign viewports are gone from the game.
  • Frame time, paired A/B with the lettering on and off (20 windows): −0.16, −0.85, +0.47, +0.14, +0.17, −2.44 ms. A control with no crisp signs moved +1.15, +0.45, −0.15 ms, so the lettering sits inside the noise. The machine was shared during these runs.
  • Per frame the cost is three unprojections per sign on screen, and one shared material per kind of light, so the labels batch.

DIV 00-J

C6 · Your route on the cutaway

tier 3 · what you did to each floor, drawn on its plate · built on art-lab

When a contract ends, the floor is boiled down into a small record kept with its LEVEL REGISTER entry. The record holds four things. First, where water stands or has left tide marks, and where soot and blast scorch are, all read off Sim's cells and scar grid in 5-pixel slices along the floor. Second, whether the power is out, and where the emergency red hangs. Third, what came down: dropped decks and breached walls. The cutaway draws that record over the floor's plate. Flooded floors hold water to a tide line. Burnt floors carry char along the floor, flame tongues up the walls and soot pooled under the ceiling, with a few embers still in it. Blacked-out floors stay dark except for the one red lamp. Collapses show as ragged holes with their rubble heaped below. Each floor's overlay is baked once into one texture, about 1.5 ms, one floor per frame as the cutaway opens. After that it costs one textured rect a frame, plus a dim and a pulse on dark floors. The newest run's damage replaces the last.

DIV 00-H

A4 · The floor remembers

Jonas picked B for every state (2026-10-01) · built

The Sim's cell state is a 0.5 m RGBA8 texture: wet, charge, heat and fuel gas. Blockwork samples it on every floor block, and the rules read the same cells, so what you see is what the rules read. The Sim binds it at floor start. The states live in value and texture, never in a new hue. Charge is a cold white, and embers are a warm white; night vision keeps both.

  • Wet: blocky mirror puddles with a lit lip and glints, damp round them.
  • Charge: single blocks flash for one 12 Hz frame, and the seams hum, shimmering block by block. The flashes are dense enough, and the hum bright enough, to hold up under the night-vision grade.
  • Heat: char with a grey ash rim round the burn, and a few glowing seams in the hottest cells.
  • Gas: drifting bands at knee height, in a 4 × 4 ordered dither.

Web cost (headless Chrome at 2560 × 1440, a cold browser per run, ?quiet, and Slipway's wall-clock frame timing). It's a paired A/B at three beats per floor, with states bound and unbound in alternating 2 s windows:

  • F73: +0.18, −0.07, −0.13 ms. With every cell at 0.8 in every channel (the worst case): +0.13, −0.30, +0.17.
  • F71: +0.07, +0.23, 0.00 ms. Worst case: +0.27, +0.06, −0.10.
Every delta is under the 0.3 ms ceiling and within the noise.

DIV 00

The answer first

recommendation and what you pick

RECOMMENDEDRoute A, Blockwork, with one pipeline for every prop

The world is built of 0.125 m blocks, as the people are built of voxels.

  • One shader gives every box a block pattern. Formwork concrete, carpet tile, steel plate, terrazzo, plywood and cladding are chosen per 0.125 m block (two character voxels, 4 screen pixels). A brick, a pier or a fracture piece carries the same blocks, so destruction matches the world with no extra work. It adds no triangles and no textures.
  • Props are modelled by code in Blender, once. props_blender.py writes 8 props now, and each exports three finishes: remeshed into blocks for route A, chamfered with baked AO for route B, flat for route C. The film uses the same source files, so Opticals' recruitment film and the game share the pipeline (see hero assets).
  • Light: the window is the one shadow-caster. It draws the mullion bars across the floor. The scene uses at most six unshadowed OmniLights, additive light pools, emissive signs and neon, and a mood per section. No LightmapGI: baked light cannot follow a floor that falls down.
  • The city: a panorama rendered from Babel's own 1,260-building data for the far city, shader-lit towers for the middle distance, and an interior-mapped facade across the street. All three are parallax layers on the camera.

About 18 h to production for Floor 70. No downloads are needed. Web risk is low: every route held 60 fps in Chrome on this machine, and route A costs no more draw calls than the greybox. Pick A, B or C (or say what to cross); I build nothing until then.

DIV 09-A

Blockwork

voxel-scale environment · procedural block materials · recommended

Every surface is sampled in 0.125 m blocks. The shader picks each block's colour from a pattern: board-formed concrete with joints and tie holes, carpet tile with a quarter-turned pile and a corporate band every 6 m, terrazzo chips, plywood grain rows, steel plate with seams and rust. A one-pixel rim is lit on top edges and dark on side edges, from the BoxMesh UVs, and draws every brick's mortar line and every fracture piece's outline. Blender props arrive already remeshed into blocks, with AO baked per block.

A-2 · A-3 · A-4Destruction and mood

A-5 · A-6 · A-7Style tiles, prop kit, signs

How destruction reads

  • Bricks, cinder cells, slabs and crates are boxes, and every box gets the block pattern in its own space, so a loose piece keeps its look while it tumbles.
  • The one-pixel rim outlines every piece in flight, which is what makes a debris storm read at 640 × 360.
  • Blender props (vending machine, kiosk) are already blocks. When one shatters, Debris spawns box chunks in the prop's key colour, and they look the same.

Shader list (Web-safe, Compatibility)

  • blockwork.gdshader: 11 patterns, rim, floor grime, local or world space, vertex colour. ALU only, no textures, no screen reads. At 230k pixels, fragment cost is not the constraint.
  • glass, pool (banded in 5 steps), card (the pano posterised to blocks), city_building, interior (quantised to 0.125 m): shared with the other routes.

Sign system

  • DotGothic16 (OFL, already shipped) at 1/30 m per font pixel, so glyphs are exact pixels. Signs are wide and chunky, and plates auto-fit their text.
  • Neon letters are built from glowing 0.125 m blocks on a stroke font, with a banded halo quad.

Prop kit

  • Done by code: vending machine, server rack, desk + chair, planter, jersey barrier, toll kiosk, turnstile, cash cage.
  • Next: water cooler, copier, filing cabinet, office sofa, lift call panel, hanging blade sign frame, fluorescent pendant, cable tray and junction box, sandbag and pallet barricade, mattress and cot for the cubicle homes.
  • Kenney Furniture Kit (CC0, on H:) can be fed through the same remesh for anything we don't write.

Cost

  • Productionise the shader and material table in Pal/LevelKit, and hook Debris/BrickWall to local space: 3 h.
  • Ten more props through props_blender.py: 5 h. Restyle Kit.sign(): 2 h.
  • Window wall, backdrop and lighting pass: 4 h. Dressing Floor 70: 3 h. Web check: 1 h.

DIV 09-B

Trim & Bake

low-poly hard surface · baked AO · trim textures

The same Blender props, chamfered (0.02–0.03 m, two segments) with Cycles AO baked into the vertex colours. Structure uses StandardMaterial with object- or world-space triplanar trim textures drawn in GDScript: formwork with drips, carpet tiles, panel seams with rivets, stone chips. They are 128 px over 4 m, so one texel covers one screen pixel. This route is the intro's pipeline, run at game scale.

How destruction reads

  • Masonry and slabs stay boxes; object-space triplanar keeps each piece's texture fixed to it.
  • Props need a pre-fractured variant or an inner "core" material (concrete aggregate, rebar ends) to avoid clean cubes coming out of chamfered shells: +4 h.

Shader list

  • StandardMaterial3D: triplanar, nearest-with-mipmaps, vertex colour for props, metallic for steel. Four 128² textures generated at load (about 200 KB of VRAM, zero download).
  • Decals are not available in Compatibility (Godot's Decal node needs Forward+/Mobile), so graffiti and hazard tape become alpha quads.

Prop kit and signs

  • The same 8 props plus the same ten more; each needs a bevel check and an AO bake (about 1 min each, headless).
  • Corporate wayfinding plates carry the Tower 3 brand; the gang signs are hand-painted plywood and lightboxes.

Cost

  • Trim set and material table: 3 h · ten more props: 6 h · fracture variants and inner material: 4 h · decals as quads: 2 h · light and backdrop: 3 h · dressing: 3 h · Web: 1 h.

DIV 09-C

Ink & Grain

flat colour · dithered light bands · one-pixel outline

A custom light() quantises light into three bands and breaks the band edges with a 4 × 4 Bayer dither on the 640 × 360 grid. Shade is tinted cool, light warm. Every mesh gets an ink hull one pixel wide: boxes grow along sign(VERTEX) so their corners stay closed, and other meshes grow along their normals. The voxel rigs are switched to the same ink shader, so the characters and the world are drawn in one hand.

How destruction reads

  • The best-separated pieces of any route, because every fragment is outlined. The risk is noise: the 131-piece burst becomes a line-drawing storm. Outlines could be dropped on pieces under 0.03 m³.

Shader list

  • ink.gdshader (custom light, Bayer), ink_outline.gdshader (next_pass, cull_front, unshaded); pools banded and dithered, the pano dithered to 5 levels.
  • Cost: each mesh draws twice (679 vs 369 draws). Fix it by merging static dressing into one mesh per room and using MultiMesh walls, which Drywall has already proposed.

Sign system and characters

  • Lettered signs, outlined plates, flat neon with dithered halos.
  • The rigs need their materials swapped (Armature's lane). It is a one-line override here, but it changes the character look Jonas approved.

Cost

  • Shaders to production: 2 h · rig material hook: 1 h · ten more props: 4 h · signs: 2 h · band and light tuning: 3 h · dressing: 3 h · batching for the outline cost: 2 h.
